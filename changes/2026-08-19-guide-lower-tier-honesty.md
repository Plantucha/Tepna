**Two reference-guide bands told the reader to seek care on the strength of a heuristic.** Closes the
last open half of `REFERENCE-GUIDE-AUDIT` dimension 3 — the per-metric honesty reading of the
`emerging`/`experimental`/`heuristic` disclaimers.

- **OxyCrash Count / Rate** (heuristic) carried internal 2/8/20-per-hour cut-points ending
  *"Severe — evaluate immediately"*, with no citation, no `no-norm-note` and a generic badge title.
  That satisfies neither (a) a published target nor (b) an explicit relative marking. The directive is
  gone and a note now names the 5 pp/30 s rule as our own choosing.
- **Positional Shifts** (heuristic) hedged correctly in prose — *"may indicate restless legs syndrome"* —
  and then hardened it in the band to *"screen for PLMS/RLS"* at an invented `>20` cut. The card
  contradicted itself, tightening as it got terser. Softened to match its own prose; note added.
- **MOS is deliberately left.** It carries the fleet's strongest directive (*"Urgent sleep specialist
  referral"*) and also its strongest disclaimer: *"Not the published McGill Oximetry Score"*, named as
  pediatric (Brouillette/Nixon), *"is not validated"*, repeated in the badge title. That is what (b)
  looks like done properly.

⚠️ **A fourth citation-presence proxy was built and thrown away first.** It flagged 69 of 76 lower-tier
banded cards — the same citation-*locality* artefact that already produced 135, 68 and ~26, because this
suite centralises citations by design. The discriminating question is not *is the band cited* but *does
it issue a clinical directive it has not earned*: 53 lower-tier bands use verdict words, only 3 tell the
reader to act, and 3 is small enough to actually read.

**The transferable result: the strongest wording had the strongest hedge.** Ranking by how alarming a
band sounds would have found MOS — the honest one — and missed both real defects.

`tools/beat-capture-recapture.mjs` — §7's capture-recapture, built and run on three shipped detectors over one host axis (18.85 min of the 2026-08-13 box night): ECG 970 beats, Verity PPG 963, O2Ring PPG 952, all ~51 bpm. **Pulse arrival time falls out as a by-product and is physiologically ordered — median 337.9 ms to the arm, 410.2 ms to the finger** — and aligning on it is mandatory rather than cosmetic: before it was handled the two optical sources matched each other 895 times and the ECG 10 times, giving `observed` = 1899 against ~970 real beats, every beat counted twice. **The estimator then REFUSES, and that refusal is the finding.** The closed form divides by m110·m101·m011 = 24·2·3 = 144 and multiplies by 9·1·12, giving m000 = 701 — 41 % of beats "missed by everything" — which would have been reported as a number had the guard not been added. It now applies the textbook adequacy rule (cell >= 5) to the six informative cells and says "the data cannot identify the undercount", explicitly not "nothing was missed". What this establishes: on a clean low-motion window the three detectors agree on 935 of 986 beats, and that agreement is exactly what destroys the estimator's power, because the cells carrying information about the unseen are single-digit. **The method has power precisely where the data is bad** — motion, perfusion, apnea — which is where the missed beats that matter for rMSSD live; quiet sleep was the wrong first test and the refusal is what said so. Getting here took four wrong diagnoses of a "0 beats" result — primitive-vs-pipeline, segment length, realm state leakage, and finally the actual cause: `detectChannel` places feet at SUB-SAMPLE fractional indices, so `t[i]` was `undefined` and a `Number.isFinite` filter silently discarded every detection. Host times are now interpolated between bracketing samples, which also keeps the sub-sample precision rather than rounding it away. Each wrong turn is recorded in the code where the reader will meet it.

Executes the threshold-margin survey this brief asked for, and the first result is that its own
prescription does not generalise.

Scanning every DSP, fusion and morph module for the shape of the original defect -- a comparison against
a scaled other quantity -- finds 22 occurrences, most of them structural loop and window bounds that
admit no margin question. The one real decision family is RR-regularity gating, which carries three
different constants: the cited Malik 20 percent used for ectopic correction, 15 percent at three sibling
sites selecting regular beats for template and MMA work, and 12 percent at a fourth site building the
normal template. Measured over 93,600 real beats these reject 3.97, 6.33 and 8.89 percent respectively,
and the two morph constants disagree about 2.56 percent of beats.

The 20 percent is a different job and is cited, so it is not an inconsistency; the 12 percent is a lone
value among four siblings in one file with no comment justifying it. But there is no gap to put a
constant in, and that is the finding: the deviation distribution is smooth, with p90 at 0.111, so the 12
percent bound sits on the shoulder of the bulk and all three constants are arbitrary points on one
continuous curve. The original EDR case had genuinely non-overlapping populations separated by a gap of
2.0; this family has none.

So the brief's rule needs the qualifier it was missing. Measuring the two populations and putting the
constant in the gap applies only where a gap exists; where the distribution is continuous the honest
record is the exclusion fraction rather than a margin. No code changed: the tighter bound is defensible
for template construction and moving it would shift every morphology golden for no measured benefit.

Closes the highest-harm item of the known-clock follow-ups: `arrivalPairOffsets` — the function deciding whether two devices sit on ONE timebase — now refuses a drawn axis on PROVENANCE rather than on magnitude. Neither existing guard is a provenance test. `independent` compares two COLUMNS, and a counter synthesised as `index x an assumed rate` at 1 s granularity has an enormous residual spread, so it reads MORE independent the coarser the fabrication. `plausibleCrystal` is `|ppm| <= 200`, a MAGNITUDE proxy that catches the O2Ring only because that axis happens to report 2730 ppm — `trio-batch.mjs`'s own comment already said the flag "says nothing about whether the DEVICE column is a clock at all", so the intent was right and the implementation was a stand-in. **Measured over 395 sidecars: one real O2Ring segment (2026-08-13, 1.72 h) reports −22.83 ppm at a 99.3 % drawn-delta share — a textbook-plausible crystal sitting between the H10's −20 and the Verity's −34 — and passes BOTH guards while having no oscillator at all.** That is the case a magnitude test cannot catch by construction. `deviceDrawn`/`drawnShare` are now carried from `hostAxis` through `trio-batch.mjs` into the device record, the Integrator gate refuses on them, the refusal names the delta concentration, and `trio-batch`'s own usable-clock count excludes them. **`arrivalPairOffsets` had NO tests at all** despite being exported and making a cross-device decision; a group now pins it, verified by removing the provenance clause — 5 of 8 assertions fail without it, decisively "with two real clocks + one drawn, exactly TWO are usable — got 3". Reported honestly at n = 1: 16 other drawn streams also pass both guards but are zero-span, where ppm = 0 trivially. Note this does NOT change `hostAxis.independent`, which remains spread-only; the parent brief's KNOWN HOLE assertion stays true and correct — the consumer has migrated off the flag rather than the flag being redefined.

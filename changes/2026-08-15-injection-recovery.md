`tools/beat-injection-recovery.mjs` — astronomy's artificial-star test, imported for beat detection, and the first bounded answer to "how many beats does the shipped detector miss?" Plants the subject's OWN averaged beat into the raw ECG at physiologically admissible positions (>=350 ms from any existing beat, so refractory rejection is never counted as a miss), re-runs the SHIPPED Pan-Tompkins, and measures recovered fraction against amplitude in units of LOCAL noise. On 25.6 min of H10 ECG (1315 baseline beats) the completeness curve has a sharp knee — 0 % at SNR <=10, **8.7 % at 20, 94.7 % at 30**, 100 % at >=40 — with **zero spurious detections at every amplitude**. Real beats on the same recording have median SNR **48.9** (p1 22.2, p5 41.0, min 10.0), with 0.61 % below SNR 20 and 2.21 % below 30; convolving distribution with curve gives roughly **1.4 % of beats at risk**. **That is an UPPER bound and the reason is structural:** injected beats sit in gaps, isolated from rhythm, while a real low-amplitude beat arrives in sequence where Pan-Tompkins' searchback reopens the window on a long RR — a mechanism built for exactly this case that an isolated plant cannot benefit from. The number must be quoted as a bound with its side named, never as the miss rate. It still matters, because a 0.5 % miss rate inflates rMSSD 114 % and 2 % inflates it 387 %: an upper bound of 1.4 % does not settle the question, it places it inside the range where the damage is large. Closing it needs the searchback-aware variant — remove a real beat and re-insert it at reduced amplitude so sequence context survives — which is a small next increment. Unlike `beat-error-recovery.mjs`, which perturbs an already-detected RR series and must therefore ASSUME a miss rate, this injects into the signal the detector actually reads, so the rate is measured. Self-test pins the curve's monotonicity and a NULL injection recovering nothing; the first version probed at SNR 20 and read a correct 0 % as a broken harness, which is recorded in the test.

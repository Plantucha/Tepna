A new SOLID-NIGHT band, `rtc`: the device's onboard RTC judged against the host clock that disciplines
it. MINOR because a band is a verdict-contract addition — every scored device's band set gains a term,
and the pinned band-set equality in `test_a_clean_h10_now_passes_EVERY_term_including_the_timebase` moves
deliberately to record it.

It is NOT the `clocks` band. `clocks` asks whether a device-vs-host comparison was recorded at all and
passes on the first `read` row carrying a parseable offset, never reading the value; `rtc` is the second
question — what the comparison found. A Polar keeps no RTC log, so its band is NOT_APPLICABLE; a ring
night that holds no log is UNKNOWN.

Every bound is pre-stated from 620 mirrored `*_RTCLOG.csv` (44 ring nights, 2883 reads) before any night
was judged, and the derivation is quoted in the brief. Two of its findings shaped the band rather than
decorating it: the offset column is two populations — a read 2–5 s after a push reports a half-applied
time (p50 5.90, max 11.9) where a read clear of one is tight (p50 0.40, max 1.9 beyond an hour) — so the
band judges reads clear of a push and states how many it set aside; and the log's own 0.1 s resolution
puts a floor under any ppm figure, so drift is null unless the span resolves the bound four times over.

The `verdict-adoption.json` note for `solid-night` was stale and is corrected here: it still said the
sample "is UNKNOWN on the timebase term, as every real night is until the residual pass lands", which
#3245 changed.

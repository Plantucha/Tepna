`tools/land-pr.mjs` — land a PR without hand-writing the polling loop, plus the measured reason the
loop exists at all.

Measured 2026-08-09: `main` moves a median 7.2 min between merges (min 1.2, max 120; only 8 of 19 recent
gaps were >= 12 min), CI is ~10-12 min across 7 required checks, and the `protect-main` ruleset sets
`required_status_checks.strict = true` — the branch must be up to date at merge time and GitHub's
auto-merge does not update it. So a PR must hold "all checks green" and "main stood still" in the same
instant, a window open well under half the time.

Every session therefore hand-writes a polling loop, and they keep being wrong in the four ways
CLAUDE.md §👥.4/4b already catalogues: a `pgrep` waiter that matches itself, a verdict read off a
truncated tail, BEHIND treated as failure when it is the race, and spinning forever on a required
context that will never arrive because a skipped matrix job reports an unexpanded literal name.

The decision is a pure function, so it is gate-backed rather than trusted: the `land-pr` group drives all
four states that need opposite responses plus the ordering that makes failure outrank BEHIND — without
`gh`, a network or a clock. Five mutants re-applied, each killed by the intended assertion.

CLAUDE.md gains §👥.5, which leads with the lever the tool does NOT provide: one PR per work-unit rather
than one per increment. The work that produced these numbers shipped as five PRs for one fix, which is
five races and ~45 min of re-running an 11-min suite over an unchanged file. A merge queue would paper
over all of it and is explicitly recorded as the wrong first reach — the self-inflicted serialisation is
the bigger term.

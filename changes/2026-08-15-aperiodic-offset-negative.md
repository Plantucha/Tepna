`tools/aperiodic-offset.mjs` and a **negative result** on target 1. The follow-up brief recorded that testing a constant offset needs an aperiodic cross-device marker and therefore a capture-protocol change. I proposed a shortcut — nature already supplies one, since turning over in bed produces a transient in BOTH devices' accelerometers at the same instant — and tested it on the real paired night (H10 chest vs Verity arm, 2026-08-13, 4.75 h overlap, ~868k samples each). **There is no usable shared transient.** Peak prominence is 0.0017–0.018 against a posture-only NULL control of 0.002, i.e. indistinguishable, and the peak RIDES THE SEARCH BOUNDARY — 3850 ms at ±4 s, 5750 at ±6 s, 9000 at ±9 s — which is what an argmax of noise does and a real lock never does. Chest and arm accelerometers do not see the same impulse well enough at this granularity over a sleep night. The brief's original claim stands and the shortcut does not exist. **The trap this found is worth more than the hypothesis was: testing an offset estimator by injecting a constant offset is VACUOUS.** A constant shift translates the whole correlation surface rigidly, so the argmax moves by exactly the injected amount regardless of whether it means anything — measured, injections of −3000/−1000/+1000/+3000 ms were recovered with error EXACTLY 0, four for four, on a night with no lock at all. Reported without prominence and the range test beside them, those numbers would have read as a clean validation of target 1; they are the most convincing instance yet of a check reporting success about something it never examined, precisely because the arithmetic is flawless. Both discriminators — prominence against a null control, and invariance of the peak to the search range — are now asserted in `--self-test`, and the tool is kept as the instrument that would detect a marker if a future capture deliberately made one.

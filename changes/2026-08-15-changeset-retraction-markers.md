Found by auditing this session's own output against `main` rather than trusting it: **two withdrawn claims survived as live assertions in PENDING changesets**, which `tools/release.mjs` folds into `CHANGELOG.md` verbatim. The corrections had been applied to the briefs, the paper and the DOCS-INDEX rows — the obvious artefacts — and the changesets were missed, so the next release would have published "the box is **Stratum 2** and therefore fails its own rate-trust gate" and "`ECG_AXIS_MIN_SPAN_MS = 2400 s` is ~an order of magnitude too permissive" as statements of fact, with their retractions appearing as separate, later entries a reader has no reason to connect. Both are now marked inline with a `[WITHDRAWN …]` prefix naming the changeset that retracts them and the one-line reason (the box is source-stratum 1 because `chronyc`'s tracking-stratum is normalised by −1; the span gate helps on 82 % of cases at 2400 s, because "is the rate resolvable" and "does correcting reduce error" are different questions). Nothing is deleted — a changeset is a historical record and the claim is part of what happened; what changes is that the correction now travels ADJACENT to the claim instead of several entries away. **The general point, which is the reason this is worth a changeset of its own:** a withdrawal is only complete when it reaches every surface that will republish the claim, and a pending changeset is such a surface precisely because it has not been published yet.

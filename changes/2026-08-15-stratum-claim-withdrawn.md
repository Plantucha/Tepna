Withdraws a second claim from the known-clock work: that the capture box is Stratum 2 and therefore **fails** its own `TIMEBASE_MAX_STRATUM = 1` rate-trust gate. Both halves are wrong. The box's own recorded decision reads `trust=disciplined absolute_ok=1 stratum=1 chrony_skew_ppm=0.004 timebase=host-disciplined` — it is host-disciplined and passes. The error: `chronyc tracking` reports `Stratum : 2` meaning THIS HOST's stratum, and `parse_chrony_tracking` normalises that to the SERVER's stratum by subtracting 1, so the value compared against the bound is 1. `host_clock.py`'s docstring warns about precisely this in capitals ("STRATUM MEANS TWO DIFFERENT THINGS AND THEY MUST NOT BE MIXED") and `test_chrony_stratum_is_normalised_to_the_SERVER_stratum` already gates it — there is no code defect and no missing test. The claim came from comparing a number read in a terminal against a constant read in a file, without ever running the function that connects them, and it was caught only by finally running the real path. Corrected in the parent brief (§2.1), the paper (Methods, now naming source-stratum 1 and the correction), the DOCS-INDEX row, and the follow-up brief — whose "re-derive `TIMEBASE_MAX_STRATUM` or document it" item is now **void**, since its premise was this error. What survives unchanged is the ratio that section actually rests on: the host's 19.5 us RMS offset is ~4 orders of magnitude tighter than the BLE jitter that immediately follows it, so PPS-disciplining buys precision the transport destroys.

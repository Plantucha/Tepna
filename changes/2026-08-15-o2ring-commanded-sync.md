Records that the O2Ring already has a commanded sync, which reframes §2.1a's co-location proposal — owner-supplied, and confirmed on hardware in `O2RING-OPCODE-SURFACE`. `0x83` drives the vibration motor (fired 5x, felt 5x, contact held) and `0x03` drains the PPG buffer, giving an exact sample COUNT between two host-timestamped reads. `0x83` beats co-location for this device because the impulse is generated INSIDE the device whose axis needs anchoring: nothing has to propagate through tissue and nothing has to be strapped together. **The larger correction is the framing.** §2.1a assumed the marker must be shared BETWEEN devices; it need not be. If each device is independently anchored to the common capture host — disciplined to 0.008 ppm on a box night — the device-to-device offset follows by subtraction. A cross-device marker is only required when there is no common host, i.e. the phone-capture case, not the box case; the co-location proposal solved a harder problem than the corpus poses. `0x03` is the stronger of the two for the axis problem and the opcode brief already said so: it converts "no timestamps at all" into host-stamped batches of known size with the residual bounded by one batch rather than accumulating across a night — which is precisely the drawn axis this work has been guarding against, removing the cause rather than refusing the symptom. Carries forward the opcode brief's own untested caveats rather than restating them as safe: whether buzzing mid-recording contaminates the ring's already-fragile motion column, the battery cost, and that `0x03` discards overflow past ~2 s with no error and no gap marker. Does NOT settle target 1 for the Polar pair, which has no commanded marker; for those, host anchoring suffices on box nights or §2.1a's co-location remains the route.

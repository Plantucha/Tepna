Corrects a wrong mechanism this session wrote into both briefs, after the owner challenged it: that target 1's marker could be "a tap artefact … a motion impulse visible in both ACC streams". A tap on one device is a LOCAL mechanical event — to reach a device on another body segment it must travel through tissue, which damps, delays and reorients it. The measurement already in the brief refutes it: a whole-body roll, far larger than any tap, produces no alignable shared transient. **The marker must be shared by CO-LOCATION, not by transmission** — strap the two devices together so they are momentarily one rigid body, deliver one sharp impulse so both accelerometers experience literally the same acceleration, then don them. The clapperboard principle; nothing has to propagate. Adds §2.1a, an executable six-step protocol (both links streaming first; devices rigidly together; sharp rap rather than hard; 3–5 impulses so the lag can be checked ACROSS them; don; repeat the sequence before doffing so two brackets separate offset from drift) with an explicit acceptance rule: `locked` true AND lag agreeing across impulses AND invariant to the search range — any one alone is satisfiable by noise. Adds §2.1b, the evidence that no natural marker exists, which is stronger than the prominence figures alone: scanning all 285 one-minute windows of the paired night gives a median prominence of 0.0092, only 5 windows above the 0.05 lock bound, and their recovered lags spread over 4.5 seconds (+200, −50, −1700, +2800, −1300, +550 ms). A real constant offset would give the SAME lag in every locking window, so the failure is not coarse windowing or a weak detector — there is nothing to find, and re-analysis will not produce it. Also notes the corpus cannot test co-location, since the devices were worn throughout and never co-located: that needs one deliberate capture.

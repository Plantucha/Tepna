`tools/known-clock-recovery.mjs` — inject a clock defect of known size into a real recording, run the shipped estimator, measure what comes back. Deterministic by contract (no `Math.random`, no `Date.now`; the one noise source is a seeded LCG), self-tested, and run over 395 arrival sidecars across 21 nights and 3 devices. Paper: `papers/known-clock-recovery.html`. Validated the stability instrument against an OUTSIDE reference first — `capture-host/allan.py` had only ever been known-answer tested against its own synthesised noise, which proves self-consistency and not correctness; against `allantools` 2024.06 it agrees to **4.78e-14** worst per-tau relative difference across all four noise types. **Results:** frequency recovery is unbiased and linear across +/-1 to +/-500 ppm at 0.03-0.09 % median relative error; a constant offset recovers as exactly 0.000 ppm on all 61 streams, so a ppm carries NO information about absolute alignment; contiguous packet loss of 50 % moves the rate by exactly 0.0000 ppm while interleaved decimation moves it up to 1115 ppm. **Two sensitivity floors found:** a 200 ms timestamp step is invisible (median ratio 1.0x), and injected wander of 0.5-5 ppm/step never moves the Allan slope off tau^-1. **The central result:** sigma_y(tau) at each recording's own span EXCEEDS the rate being quoted for 100 % of streams under 300 s, 94 % under an hour and 42 % beyond it — the estimator's accuracy and the quantity's resolvability are independent questions and only the first had been asked. **[WITHDRAWN 2026-08-14 — see `2026-08-14-span-gate-withdrawn.md`: the gate is doing net good where it stands, helping on 82 % of cases at 2400 s.]** This changeset originally inferred that `ECG_AXIS_MIN_SPAN_MS = 2400 s` is ~an order of magnitude too permissive; that conflated whether a rate is RESOLVABLE with whether correcting REDUCES ERROR. Also records two bugs found in the new tool itself by measuring rather than reasoning: a guard on `stability.ok`, a field that does not exist, which made the whole first corpus run report wander as "(none)" — the repo's own named failure class, committed by the instrument built to hunt it — and a sign error in the self-test that failed at exactly -198.7 %, the injected sign doubled.

Paper Draft v3 adds §3.2b: the second route to a constant offset also fails, and the obvious test for it cannot. §3.2 already showed the shipped estimator is blind to constant offset BY CONSTRUCTION; the standard alternative is to align an aperiodic feature, and we asked whether a night already contains one — a subject turning over produces a transient in both a chest and an arm accelerometer at the same instant. On 4.75 h of paired overnight ACC (~868,000 samples per device) it is not usable: peak prominence 0.0017–0.018 against a posture-only null control of 0.002, and the peak MOVES WITH THE SEARCH RANGE (3850 ms at ±4 s, 5750 at ±6 s, 9000 at ±9 s), which a genuine lock never does. **The transferable result is the validation trap.** The natural test — inject a known constant offset, check it is recovered — cannot fail, because a constant shift translates the entire cross-correlation surface rigidly and the peak moves by exactly the injected amount whether or not it locates anything: four injections were recovered with error exactly 0 from a method with no lock at all. Reported alone those numbers are indistinguishable from a validated alignment and MORE persuasive than a noisy correct result. The paper now states that an injected constant offset is not evidence for a correlation-based offset estimator, and names the two things that are: prominence against a null control, and invariance of the peak to the search range.

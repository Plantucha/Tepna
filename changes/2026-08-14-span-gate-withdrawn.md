Withdraws a claim this session shipped one release earlier: that `ECG_AXIS_MIN_SPAN_MS = 2400 s` is "roughly an order of magnitude too permissive". It is not, and the correction is recorded in the brief and the paper rather than deleted, because it shipped in both. The error was conflating two questions that a single ratio cannot separate: whether a rate is **resolved** (|ppm| vs sigma_y — quotable as distinguishable from zero, which under an hour it is not) and whether applying the correction **reduces error** (|estimate − truth| vs |0 − truth|). A span gate governs the second. A point estimate can sit below its own noise floor and still be much closer to the truth than assuming zero. Measured directly, taking the full-span rate as truth on streams whose full-span estimate is itself resolved and then truncating the same stream: at the shipped 2400 s gate the correction **helps on 82 %** of cases and more than halves the median error (8.41 ppm corrected vs 22.27 uncorrected); at 4800 s and 9600 s it helps 100 %. The gate is doing net good where it stands; a raise to 4800 s would remove the 18 % harm cases (worst: Verity truth −34.3 ppm estimated at −77.3) at the cost of refusing correction on fragments it currently improves — defensible, and a far smaller claim. n is 9–11 because truth requires a resolved full-span estimate. `HOSTAXIS-STABILITY` made a claim of this shape and withdrew it as "marginal, not wrong"; this work re-opened it on stronger evidence and then independently reproduced the reason it was withdrawn — the withdrawal was correct. The sigma_y result is untouched: it licenses a caveat on QUOTING a ppm, not a change to a gate that governs APPLYING one. Adds the two harnesses that produced this (`experiments/known-clock/span-sweep.mjs`, `does-correction-help.mjs`), including a 162-point within-stream span sweep showing sigma_y halves as span doubles and resolution crossing 50 % between 4800 s and 9600 s.

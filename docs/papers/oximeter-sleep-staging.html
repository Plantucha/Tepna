Draft v2 · October 2026 · Skeleton 2026-10-04; results v1 2026-10-22; results v2 2026-10-31 · Inputs: nine pooled cohorts (open + NSRR), 14 695 nights, feature sets oxy1 (466 columns) + pdx (175 pulse-dynamics bases); ecg2r, rsp (ECG and breathing lines), oxy1p + pdxp (pulse from the PPG waveform); nl extracted, never fitted · bands pre-stated in briefs/STAGE-LAB-2026-09-27-BRIEF.md §§5–50 before each run · nothing here is a published rate until the owner opens the P5 gate

Abstract

Background. A fingertip ring oximeter reports three signals at 1 Hz: SpO₂, pulse rate and a movement byte. The best published figure for staging from the first two is κ 0.73–0.78 on 17 303 polysomnograms with a whole-night network (Vaquerizo-Villar et al., 2025); a proprietary ring stager reports κ 0.39 on a sealed Challenge-2018 holdout. Question. How much of the night can those three signals stage, what part of it a stack of published formulas can carry, and how the two compare blind. Methods. Nine cohorts (14 695 nights: SHHS-1/2, MESA, CFS, MrOS, CAP, UCDDB, ISRUC) harmonised to what the ring reports; 466 named features plus 175 pulse-dynamics bases; three learners on identical subject-grouped folds — gradient-boosted trees, a whole-night sequence network used only as a control, and a literature stack (named terms → auto-context → linear-chain CRF) fitted to labels; every band stated before its run; Challenge 2018 never fitted, scored blind; leave-one-cohort-out for the four NSRR cohorts; a half-night label shift that must collapse κ as the plant. Results. The control reaches pooled κ 0.711 (0.794 three-class) and 0.573 blind on Challenge; holding out MESA, MrOS or CFS costs 0.006–0.026 and SHHS-1 0.045, so the blind gap of 0.14 is site and scoring, not cohort. Adding 4 646 nights moved the control +0.009. The literature stack rose from 0.482 to 0.558 when the pulse-dynamics bases were added (+0.079 [+0.078, +0.081] paired on 15 689 nights, against a pre-stated +0.005 to +0.02) and to 0.570 with every cached column as a line term; blind 0.392 against the proprietary stager's 0.390. An oracle that fits each night's own intercepts and slopes lifts the stack to 0.616, but that calibration is not predictable from the night (recovery ≤ 14 %), not carried by the same person's other night years apart (−0.03 to −0.06), and worth ≤ +0.03 from a user's bed and wake times; a second formula that predicts each night's adapters from its label-free statistics recovers +0.041 of it (stack 0.597, blind 0.407, past the proprietary 0.390); distillation from the control hurt twice. Draft v2 (2026-10-31). STAGES' thirteen clinics read the control at 0.533 blind, and training on sister clinics did not repair a held-out one (−0.018 to +0.018). The ECG net reads 0.681 against a five-scorer consensus (DOD-O). After a data-integrity rework every line was re-run on corrected data (Table 6). The nets hold (oximetry 0.705, ECG + breathing 0.702; about 0.57 on blind Challenge), and no formula passes a ship band blind (oximetry formula 0.374 on Challenge, 0.242 on DREAMT's wrist pulse). A wearable-only net reaches 0.658 in the pool and 0.41 on DREAMT. Conclusion. The formula route was capped by missing terms, not by its linear form: with the pulse-dynamics bases it clears the pre-stated shipping line (0.50) and matches the proprietary comparator blind. What separates it from the control is a this-night calibration that only labels on that night supply; the control itself already reads the whole night and sits at the lower edge of the published 0.73–0.78.

0. Layman overview (delete before submission)

A sleep lab decides whether you are awake, in light sleep, deep sleep or dreaming by reading your brain waves. A ring on your finger cannot see your brain; it sees how much oxygen is in your blood, how fast your pulse is, and whether your hand moved. We asked how far those three signals can go. We built the best model we could as a yardstick, then tried to reach the same result with formulas other scientists have already published, because formulas can be read, checked and shipped on a tiny device. Every target was written down before the computer ran, and two groups of nights were kept locked away so that the final numbers could not have been tuned to them.

1. Introduction

Two human scorers reading the same polysomnogram agree at κ ≈ 0.83 (measured here on ISRUC, Khalighi et al., 2016). A wearable has none of the channels they read. The question of this paper is therefore not whether a ring equals a lab, but how much of the night a ring's three 1-Hz signals contain, and whether that information can be expressed in formulas rather than in a black box.

Comparators. A proprietary stager for the same ring family reports κ 0.367 live / 0.390 offline on a sealed 134-night Challenge-2018 holdout (published figures only; its model and outputs were never used, per its licence §3g). Sridhar et al. (2020) reached κ 0.66 from instantaneous heart rate alone with a whole-night CNN on SHHS/MESA. Vaquerizo-Villar et al. (2025) reached κ 0.73–0.78 from 1-Hz pulse rate and SpO₂ on 17 303 PSGs and published the frequency bands their network attends to, which this paper adopts as formula terms. From the PPG waveform of a PSG oximeter, Kotzen et al. (2023) reached κ 0.75 held-out and 0.74 on an external database after transfer, and Attia et al. (2025) showed that training on several sources at once lifts out-of-distribution κ by up to 21 %; from a wrist device, Radha et al. (2021) reached 0.65 on 60 healthy participants with ECG-to-PPG transfer. Ganglberger et al. (2024) added a scorability model that predicts, from signal quality and model confidence, how far an automated staging can be trusted. The human ceilings these are read against: 82.6 % inter-scorer agreement over more than 2 500 scorers, with N1 at 63.0 % and N3 at 67.4 % (Rosenberg & Van Hout, 2013), and κ 0.76 under AASM rules against 0.68 under R&K between experts (Danker-Hopfe et al., 2009) — the latter is why this paper reports cohorts scored under the two standards in separate rows.

Design. Three learners on one feature matrix and identical folds: trees as the floor, a sequence network as the ceiling and teacher ("control", never shipped), and a stack of published formulas as the candidate for release. The owner's rule "train on everything in-house, publish only what the formulas carry" is what the design implements.

2.2 Harmonisation to the ring

SpO₂ is reduced to an 8-s moving mean at 1 Hz in whole percent, which is what the ring writes (one byte per second per signal). Pulse rate is the oximeter's own 1-Hz rate where the PSG has one; otherwise 60/RR from ECG beats found by the suite's Pan–Tompkins detector (Pan & Tompkins, 1985; validated at Se/PPV ≥ 99 % on MIT-BIH, SLPDB and NSRDB, Table S1) with a 4-s mean. Motion is the per-second standard deviation of wrist acceleration magnitude; a 1-bit "moved this second" flag (> 0.02 g) is carried beside it, as the poorest signal the ring could report. Every signal is standardised per night (median/IQR).

2.3 Feature matrix (feature set oxy1, 466 columns)

Multi-scale statistics (1–10 min), desaturation events (the ≥ 3 % criterion of the AASM 2012 rules, Berry et al., 2012; the detector is ours, and implementations differ, Terrill, 2020), SpO₂ and pulse-rate band powers including the three PR bands of Vaquerizo-Villar et al. (2025), pulse-rate statistics on the 1-Hz device rate (the mean, SD and successive-difference features that paper names; not HRV in the Task Force (1996) sense, and only after the RR-interval features of Redmond & Heneghan, 2006), and movement statistics after Cole et al. (1992) and Sadeh et al. (1994) — analogues of their inputs, not their scoring algorithms — each with ±2/±10/±30-epoch context. A literature check of 2026-10-30 re-attributed every term to what its source actually defines; the term-by-term verdicts, the terms retired (a Cole–Kripke-weighted window, 5-min VLF, posture turns) and the quantities added (a ceiling-baseline event detector shared with OxyDex, cardiopulmonary coupling with the parameters of Thomas et al., 2005, and per-epoch signal-quality fractions after the scorability idea of Ganglberger et al., 2024) are in docs/STAGE-LAB-FORMULAS.md.

Pulse-dynamics bases (pdx, brief §31, added 2026-10-20): 35 quantities of the pulse-rate and SpO₂ series that a linear formula cannot derive from the oxy1 statistics — lag-1/5/10 autocorrelation, DFA α₁/α₂, sample and permutation entropy, turning-point rate, Teager energy, Higuchi dimension, SD1/SD2, the HF spectral peak and its drift, pulse surges and the time since the last surge, SpO₂ step, desaturation duration and burden, resaturation slope, SpO₂–pulse cross-correlation and lag, the pulse response after movement, and per-channel missingness — each raw and at ±2, ±10, ±30 epochs. Night-level constants (nl, 8 columns: pulse median/IQR/trend, SpO₂ median/5th percentile, ODI-3, motion fraction, time in bed) bypass the per-night normalisation and are the input of the night-level formula arm (§39, pending).

2.4 Learners

  • Trees: histogram gradient boosting (Friedman, 2001), NaN-aware, 200 rounds, lr 0.1, 31 leaves.
  • Control: dilated temporal convolutions (receptive field ≈ 2 h) with a bidirectional GRU over the whole night, class-weighted cross-entropy, epoch selected on an inner 10 % split; never shipped, used as ceiling and teacher.
  • Literature stack: softmax over named terms (line + hinge), fitted by L-BFGS; auto-context (Tu & Bai, 2010) on out-of-fold posteriors; a linear-chain CRF (Lafferty et al., 2001 — the one-dimensional Potts chain, Potts, 1952) over out-of-fold unaries with the unary scale fixed at 1. Variants: physiology-fixed coefficient signs; distillation from the control (Hinton et al., 2015); a bridge of long-range context terms, cumulative stage mix and an ultradian phase by matched filter; symbolic regression on the residual to the control.
  • Decoding: HMM Viterbi over scaled likelihoods (Rabiner, 1989) for trees and control; the CRF's own Viterbi for the stack.

2.5 Pre-registration and plants

Each unit's expected range, pass line and reading rule were written in briefs/STAGE-LAB-2026-09-27-BRIEF.md before the unit ran (§§5–20); measured numbers were appended beside them, including every failed expectation. The gate for all units: pooled κ ≥ 0.415, half the human inter-scorer κ (0.829). Plant P1: labels shifted by half a night must collapse κ below half its value, or the run is void. Folds are a hash of the subject key (SHHS1/SHHS2 visits of one person share a fold); Challenge 2018 and ISRUC are never in any fit.

4. Discussion

(i) Where the gap lives. The control beats the stack by 0.15 pooled and 0.18 blind. The oracle of §32 locates most of it: with each night's own intercepts and per-term slopes the stack reaches 0.616, within 0.1 of the control, and its 9 % of failed nights (κ < 0.2) vanish. The three-class readout (§38a) says the remaining errors are not dominated by one boundary — the gain from collapsing light and deep is the same +0.08–0.09 on every model, as in Korkalainen et al. (2020).

(ii) What label-free calibration recovers. A second formula predicting each night's adapters from its label-free statistics recovers +0.041 of the oracle's +0.25 on the full basis (v32 F2), inside its pre-stated +0.03 to +0.08 and enough to take the stack past the proprietary comparator blind. The rest is not reachable label-free: the retrofit diagnostic on a 52-term basis read only 14 % (§40); the same person's adapter from a visit years earlier is worse than none (§41); and a user's bed and wake times reach +0.03 at most (§42). Distillation from the control lost twice before that (§15, §19). The calibration is a this-night quantity that only labels on the night supply. The sequence control already reads the whole night (a bidirectional GRU over the full recording) and sits at 0.711, the lower edge of the 0.726–0.779 that Vaquerizo-Villar et al. (2025) report with a 12-hour U-Net on the same two signals; the remaining gap to that result is small and not an architecture-class difference, and more nights alone do not close it (+0.009 for +50 %, §30). For the formula the gap to the control is this calibration; for the control the published band is within reach of volume, tuning and the padded 12-hour input.

(iii) Terms, not capacity. The stack's long plateau at 0.49–0.50 ended when the pulse-dynamics bases were added (+0.075 against a pre-stated +0.005 to +0.02), while the control gained +0.005 from the same bases. A ceiling the control does not share is a feature problem; the stack now clears the pre-stated shipping line and matches the proprietary comparator blind (0.392 vs 0.390).

(iv) Site, not cohort. Holding out an NSRR cohort costs 0.006–0.026 (0.045 for the 1990s SHHS-1), reproducing the published "< 3 %" leave-one-cohort-out drop; the Challenge gap is 0.14, the same drop Sridhar et al. (2020) crossed (0.66 → 0.55). STAGES' thirteen clinics (§43) answered whether sister sites repair it: they do not. The control scored 0.533 blind over STAGES, and training on the other twelve clinics moved a held-out clinic by −0.018 to +0.018. Two clinics (STLK, STNF) carry the deficit, and a 0–29 s scoring-grid offset at STNF is not the cause (§52.8). The shift is per clinic: device, scorers or population. A five-scorer consensus reference reads the ECG net at 0.681 (DOD-O, §44), so part of every single-scorer blind number is the reference's own noise.

(v) What can ship. The stack's coefficients are results of the analysis and publishable under the NSRR agreement; the control's weights are trained on NSRR data and remain a user-loaded, unshipped artefact. For a night without a PSG oximeter (§§45–48): the pulse rate derived from the PPG waveform is not worse than the device's own rate (+0.038 paired). A wearable-only net reaches 0.658 in the pool but 0.41 on DREAMT's wrist nights, in the band where the wrist costs the difference. Transfer from ECG pre-training does not close that gap (+0.015, CI including 0). On the §46 data no formula yet passes a ship band blind, which is what v51's three switches and the v52 rebuild are for.

6. Sample size & statistical power

With 14 695 nights (≈ 14 million epochs) the night-bootstrap 95 % CI on pooled κ is ±0.004, and a paired difference between two models on the same nights resolves to ±0.001 (measured: +0.0093 [+0.0083, +0.0103], brief §30), so any difference ≥ 0.01 between learners on the same folds is resolvable; the pre-stated reading rules use 0.01 as the smallest difference worth a claim. The blind cohorts (994 and 127 nights) resolve ≈ ±0.015 and ±0.04 respectively; DREAMT (97–100 nights) and DOD-O (56) resolve no better than ±0.04, so on them a paired difference under about 0.03 is a CI through zero, as in §§46–48. From v51 on, CIs are by subject-cluster bootstrap (§52.4), wider than the night bootstrap where subjects contribute several nights.

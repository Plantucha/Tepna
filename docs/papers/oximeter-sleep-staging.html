Sleep staging from a fingertip oximeter's three signals: a pre-registered ceiling, a sequence control, and a stack of published formulas scored blind

Michal Planicka  ·  corresponding author — Tepna Project

OxyDex node (SpO₂ · pulse rate · motion), Tepna physiological-signal suite

Draft v0 · October 2026 · Skeleton written 2026-10-04 while v21/v22 run · Inputs: ten open and NSRR cohorts, 9 100 nights, feature set oxy1 · bands pre-stated in briefs/STAGE-LAB-2026-09-27-BRIEF.md §§5–20 before each run · nothing here is a published rate until the owner opens the P5 gate

Abstract

Background. A fingertip ring oximeter reports three signals at 1 Hz: SpO₂, pulse rate and a movement byte. The best published figure for staging from the first two is κ 0.73–0.78 on 17 303 polysomnograms with a whole-night network (Vaquerizo-Villar et al., 2025); a proprietary ring stager reports κ 0.39 on a sealed Challenge-2018 holdout. Question. How much of the night can those three signals stage, what part of it a stack of published formulas can carry, and how the two compare blind. Methods. Ten cohorts (9 100 nights) harmonised to what the ring reports; 466 named features; three learners on identical subject-grouped folds — gradient-boosted trees, a whole-night sequence network used only as a control, and a literature stack (named terms → auto-context → linear-chain CRF) fitted to labels; every band stated before its run; Challenge 2018 and ISRUC never fitted, scored blind; a half-night label shift that must collapse κ as the plant. Results. PENDING — control, trees, stack, bridge and symbolic-regression cells fill from the brief when v21/v22 land; the external blind number fills after the third-party blind run. Conclusion. PENDING.

0. Layman overview (delete before submission)

A sleep lab decides whether you are awake, in light sleep, deep sleep or dreaming by reading your brain waves. A ring on your finger cannot see your brain; it sees how much oxygen is in your blood, how fast your pulse is, and whether your hand moved. We asked how far those three signals can go. We built the best model we could as a yardstick, then tried to reach the same result with formulas other scientists have already published, because formulas can be read, checked and shipped on a tiny device. Every target was written down before the computer ran, and two groups of nights were kept locked away so that the final numbers could not have been tuned to them.

1. Introduction

Two human scorers reading the same polysomnogram agree at κ ≈ 0.83 (measured here on ISRUC, Khalighi et al., 2016). A wearable has none of the channels they read. The question of this paper is therefore not whether a ring equals a lab, but how much of the night a ring's three 1-Hz signals contain, and whether that information can be expressed in formulas rather than in a black box.

Comparators. A proprietary stager for the same ring family reports κ 0.367 live / 0.390 offline on a sealed 134-night Challenge-2018 holdout (published figures only; its model and outputs were never used, per its licence §3g). Sridhar et al. (2020) reached κ 0.66 from instantaneous heart rate alone with a whole-night CNN on SHHS/MESA. Vaquerizo-Villar et al. (2025) reached κ 0.73–0.78 from 1-Hz pulse rate and SpO₂ on 17 303 PSGs and published the frequency bands their network attends to, which this paper adopts as formula terms. From the PPG waveform of a PSG oximeter, Kotzen et al. (2023) reached κ 0.75 held-out and 0.74 on an external database after transfer, and Attia et al. (2025) showed that training on several sources at once lifts out-of-distribution κ by up to 21 %; from a wrist device, Radha et al. (2021) reached 0.65 on 60 healthy participants with ECG-to-PPG transfer. Ganglberger et al. (2024) added a scorability model that predicts, from signal quality and model confidence, how far an automated staging can be trusted. The human ceilings these are read against: 82.6 % inter-scorer agreement over more than 2 500 scorers, with N1 at 63.0 % and N3 at 67.4 % (Rosenberg & Van Hout, 2013), and κ 0.76 under AASM rules against 0.68 under R&K between experts (Danker-Hopfe et al., 2009) — the latter is why this paper reports cohorts scored under the two standards in separate rows.

Design. Three learners on one feature matrix and identical folds: trees as the floor, a sequence network as the ceiling and teacher ("control", never shipped), and a stack of published formulas as the candidate for release. The owner's rule "train on everything in-house, publish only what the formulas carry" is what the design implements.

2.2 Harmonisation to the ring

SpO₂ is reduced to an 8-s moving mean at 1 Hz in whole percent, which is what the ring writes (one byte per second per signal). Pulse rate is the oximeter's own 1-Hz rate where the PSG has one; otherwise 60/RR from ECG beats found by the suite's Pan–Tompkins detector (Pan & Tompkins, 1985; validated at Se/PPV ≥ 99 % on MIT-BIH, SLPDB and NSRDB, Table S1) with a 4-s mean. Motion is the per-second standard deviation of wrist acceleration magnitude; a 1-bit "moved this second" flag (> 0.02 g) is carried beside it, as the poorest signal the ring could report. Every signal is standardised per night (median/IQR).

2.3 Feature matrix (feature set oxy1, 466 columns)

Multi-scale statistics (1–10 min), desaturation events (the ≥ 3 % criterion of the AASM 2012 rules, Berry et al., 2012; the detector is ours, and implementations differ, Terrill, 2020), SpO₂ and pulse-rate band powers including the three PR bands of Vaquerizo-Villar et al. (2025), pulse-rate statistics on the 1-Hz device rate (the mean, SD and successive-difference features that paper names; not HRV in the Task Force (1996) sense, and only after the RR-interval features of Redmond & Heneghan, 2006), and movement statistics after Cole et al. (1992) and Sadeh et al. (1994) — analogues of their inputs, not their scoring algorithms — each with ±2/±10/±30-epoch context. A literature check of 2026-10-30 re-attributed every term to what its source actually defines; the term-by-term verdicts, the terms retired (a Cole–Kripke-weighted window, 5-min VLF, posture turns) and the quantities added (a ceiling-baseline event detector shared with OxyDex, cardiopulmonary coupling with the parameters of Thomas et al., 2005, and per-epoch signal-quality fractions after the scorability idea of Ganglberger et al., 2024) are in docs/STAGE-LAB-FORMULAS.md.

2.4 Learners

  • Trees: histogram gradient boosting (Friedman, 2001), NaN-aware, 200 rounds, lr 0.1, 31 leaves.
  • Control: dilated temporal convolutions (receptive field ≈ 2 h) with a bidirectional GRU over the whole night, class-weighted cross-entropy, epoch selected on an inner 10 % split; never shipped, used as ceiling and teacher.
  • Literature stack: softmax over named terms (line + hinge), fitted by L-BFGS; auto-context (Tu & Bai, 2010) on out-of-fold posteriors; a linear-chain CRF (Lafferty et al., 2001 — the one-dimensional Potts chain, Potts, 1952) over out-of-fold unaries with the unary scale fixed at 1. Variants: physiology-fixed coefficient signs; distillation from the control (Hinton et al., 2015); a bridge of long-range context terms, cumulative stage mix and an ultradian phase by matched filter; symbolic regression on the residual to the control.
  • Decoding: HMM Viterbi over scaled likelihoods (Rabiner, 1989) for trees and control; the CRF's own Viterbi for the stack.

2.5 Pre-registration and plants

Each unit's expected range, pass line and reading rule were written in briefs/STAGE-LAB-2026-09-27-BRIEF.md before the unit ran (§§5–20); measured numbers were appended beside them, including every failed expectation. The gate for all units: pooled κ ≥ 0.415, half the human inter-scorer κ (0.829). Plant P1: labels shifted by half a night must collapse κ below half its value, or the run is void. Folds are a hash of the subject key (SHHS1/SHHS2 visits of one person share a fold); Challenge 2018 and ISRUC are never in any fit.

5. Reproducibility

# features (one tag per feature set; a changed layout never mixes with an older cache)
node tools/stage-lab.mjs features --harmonize --blocks spo2,pulse,motion --tag oxy1 --cohort <c> --dir <dir> --cache <cache> --workers 4
# trees and control on identical subject-grouped folds
node tools/stage-lab.mjs bench --cache <cache> --cohorts <ten> --features oxy1 --group-subjects --model gbdt --fold <f>
python seqnet.py --cache <cache> --cohorts <ten> --features oxy1 --group-subjects --fold <f> --save-proba <dir>
# literature stack (label-fitted, signed, distilled, bridge) and the residual search
python litstack.py --cache <cache> --cohorts <ten> --features oxy1 --group-subjects --fold <f> [--signed] [--distill <dir>] [--bridge]
python symreg.py --cache <cache> --control <dir> --stack <dir> --out <dir>
  • Every job runs under an 8 GB cgroup cap and logs its full-size peak memory; trainers read one night at a time.
  • Fold assignment: 32-bit hash of the subject key modulo 5; deterministic, so added cohorts never move existing nights.
  • Human ceiling: ISRUC scorer 1 vs scorer 2, κ 0.829, computed by stage-lab.mjs on the same epochs.
  • Verdicts are emitted as tepna.verdict/1 with the pre-stated criterion embedded.

6. Sample size & statistical power

With 9 100 nights (≈ 9 million epochs) the night-bootstrap 95 % CI on pooled κ is ±0.004 (measured: 0.608 [0.604, 0.612] for the control, brief §17), so any difference ≥ 0.01 between learners on the same folds is resolvable; the pre-stated reading rules use 0.01 as the smallest difference worth a claim. The blind cohorts (994 and 127 nights) resolve ≈ ±0.015 and ±0.04 respectively.
